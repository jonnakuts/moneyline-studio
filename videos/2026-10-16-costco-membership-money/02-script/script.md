# The store that barely profits

Costco doesn't hide any of this.
It's all in the yearly report it files with the US government.
Costco began in Seattle in 1983. [F:cost-founded-1983]
Today it runs 939 warehouses around the world. [F:cost-warehouses-2026]
In its 2026 fiscal year, Costco sold about $297 billion of goods. [F:cost-net-sales-fy2026]
That's groceries, TVs, tires, fuel, glasses, all of it.
To buy those goods from its suppliers, it paid about $264 billion. [F:cost-merch-costs-fy2026]
So of everything you spend, only about 11% is left once Costco has paid for the goods. [F:cost-gross-margin-fy2026]
That leftover is called the gross margin.
And Costco says itself that it runs on much lower margins than most other retailers. [F:cost-low-margin-model]
[BEAT: the eleven dollars shrink]
Out of that small slice, Costco still has to pay its staff, run its warehouses, and keep the lights on.
Those running costs take about $9 of every hundred dollars you spend. [F:cost-sga-share-sales-fy2026]
So what's left from selling goods?
Under $2. [F:cost-goods-profit-per-100-fy2026]
<!-- visual: a hundred-dollar stack; supplier takes most, staff and buildings take the rest, two coins remain -->
Under two dollars of profit for every hundred dollars of shopping. [F:cost-goods-profit-per-100-fy2026]
That's thin.
If Costco were only a shop, it would be a very ordinary business.
So where does the rest of the profit come from?

# The card

From the one thing in your wallet that has nothing on its shelf.
In America, a basic Costco membership costs $65 a year. [F:cost-fee-goldstar-us]
At the end of its 2026 fiscal year, Costco had about 84.1 million paid members. [F:cost-paid-members-2026]
Add the free second cards for people in the same household, and it's about 150 million cardholders. [F:cost-cardholders-2026]
All those fees added up to $5.9 billion in one year. [F:cost-member-fees-fy2026]
And that number keeps climbing.
Two years earlier, it was about $4.8 billion. [F:cost-member-fees-fy2024]
The year after that, about $5.3 billion. [F:cost-member-fees-fy2025]
<!-- visual: three cards stacked like a staircase -->
[BEAT: the comparison]
Now compare that with Costco's operating profit, the money it makes from running the business, before interest and tax.
That was $11.7 billion. [F:cost-op-income-fy2026]
So the membership fees are as big as about half of all that profit. [F:cost-fee-share-opinc-fy2026]
To be fair, Costco doesn't split its profit by where it comes from.
This is a comparison of sizes, not an official breakdown.
But it tells you what the whole business is built around.
Here's why that matters.
Unlike a TV, a membership doesn't need a box shipped or a shelf stocked.
So take the fees away, and on paper the operating profit drops to about $5.8 billion. [F:cost-opinc-ex-fees-fy2026]
<!-- visual: two stacked bars; the fee block lifts off and the bar halves -->
That's the trick hiding in plain sight.
Costco sells you the goods at almost no profit.
And sells you the right to shop there at a very healthy one.
You might be thinking, isn't that just a subscription?
Sort of, but with a twist.
With most subscriptions, you pay for the thing itself, like shows or music.
At Costco, you pay for the right to buy things at almost no markup.
So the better the deal on the shelves, the more the subscription is worth.
The shop and the card aren't two businesses.
They're one machine, and each half feeds the other.
Costco says it plainly in its own report: the membership format is integral to its business and profitability. [F:cost-membership-format]

# Why cheaper means more money

But a fee only works if people keep paying it.
So here's the loop that holds the whole thing together.
Low prices make the membership feel worth it.
People who feel it's worth it renew.
And renewals are the profit.
How many actually renew?
In the US and Canada, the renewal rate was 92.3%. [F:cost-renewal-usca-2026]
Worldwide, it was nearly 90%. [F:cost-renewal-world-2026]
<!-- visual: one card travelling round a calendar ring, nine out of ten come back -->
Think about what that means.
Roughly nine out of ten members decide, every single year, to pay again. [F:cost-renewal-world-2026,cost-renewal-usca-2026]
If Costco raised its prices on goods to squeeze out more profit, the deal would feel worse.
Renewals could slip.
And the most valuable part of the business would start to leak.
So Costco keeps prices low on purpose. [F:cost-low-margin-model]
The cheap prices aren't the cost of the business.
They're the marketing for the membership.
[BEAT: money before it's earned]
There's one more quiet advantage.
Members pay for the whole year up front.
But Costco counts that fee as income slowly, evenly across the year. [F:cost-fees-deferred]
At the end of its 2026 fiscal year, Costco was holding about $3 billion of fees it had collected but not yet counted as earned. [F:cost-deferred-fees-2026]
And it goes further with its suppliers.
Costco says it often sells goods before it even has to pay for them. [F:cost-sell-before-pay]
So your money arrives early, and Costco's bills arrive late.

# The hot dog promise

You can see the whole strategy in one famous item.
The food court hot dog and soda.
It has cost $1.50 since 1985, according to news reports. [F:cost-hotdog-1985]
In 2024, when people started asking if that price would finally go up, Costco's finance chief said on an earnings call that the price is safe. [F:cost-hotdog-1985]
Costco doesn't report how much it makes or loses on hot dogs.
But the point isn't the hot dog.
The point is the promise.
A price that never moves tells every member that Costco is on their side.
And members who believe that keep renewing.

# The members who pay more

[BEAT: re-hook]
But here's the part most people miss.
The cheapest membership isn't where the story ends.
Costco also sells an upgrade called Executive membership.
In America, it costs $130 a year, double the basic fee. [F:cost-fee-exec-us,cost-fee-goldstar-us]
In return, members get back a 2% reward on qualifying purchases, up to $1,250 a year. [F:cost-exec-reward-rate,cost-exec-reward-max]
So why would Costco pay people to shop?
Look at who these members are.
At the end of fiscal 2026, there were about 42.3 million Executive members. [F:cost-exec-members-2026]
That's about half of all paid members. [F:cost-exec-share-members-2026]
But they made about 75.2% of Costco's sales worldwide. [F:cost-exec-sales-share-2026]
<!-- visual: member figures split half and half; the sales bar behind them splits three to one -->
Spend about $3,250 a year, and the reward pays back the extra fee. [F:cost-exec-breakeven-spend]
Shop more than that, and the upgrade feels free.
So the people who shop the most lock themselves in the hardest.
In 2026, those rewards cost Costco about $3.4 billion, taken straight off its sales. [F:cost-exec-rewards-2026]
That's a lot to give away.
But it buys Costco its biggest and most loyal shoppers.

# What could break it

So is this model unbreakable?
Not quite, and Costco knows exactly where the pressure is.
In September 2024, it raised the basic fee in the US and Canada by $5. [F:cost-fee-goldstar-increase]
The Executive fee went from $120 to $130. [F:cost-fee-exec-us-old,cost-fee-exec-us]
In fiscal 2026, fee revenue grew 11%. [F:cost-fee-growth-fy2026]
But only about 30% of that growth came from the higher price. [F:cost-fee-incr-share-2026]
The rest came from more members joining and more of them upgrading.
That's the real engine.
And notice the gap between the two renewal rates.
Costco says its worldwide rate is pulled down by fast growth in some newer countries, and by memberships sold online, which renew a little less often. [F:cost-renewal-intl-online-lower]
So as Costco grows into new places, keeping that loyalty is not automatic.
Raising the fee is easy.
Keeping people happy enough to pay it is the hard part.
If renewals ever fall, the profit falls with them, because so much of it rests on that one card.
Costco's own report says it: sustaining high renewal rates materially influences its profitability. [F:cost-renewal-drives-profit]

So if you ever want to know how Costco is really doing, don't watch the shelves.
Watch the renewal rate.
