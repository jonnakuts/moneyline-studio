# Pitch: How Costco Actually Makes Money

Status: draft for Gate A · 2026-10-16

## Promise
By the end, the viewer understands why Costco can sell almost everything near cost and still be hugely
profitable: the yearly membership fee is the real product, and the warehouse exists to make people renew.

## The one viewer
A 20–35-year-old anywhere in the world who has shopped at Costco or heard of it (giant packs, cheap hot dogs,
cheap rotisserie chicken). Knows nothing about accounting. Has never looked at a company's annual report.

## Cold-open idea (to verify)
Start inside the cart: "Everything in this cart, Costco sold you at a tiny markup. Almost all of its profit
came from one small plastic card." The lead to verify: membership fee income compared to Costco's total
yearly profit. If the numbers don't show this clearly, the cold open changes.

## Curiosity chain
1. If Costco's markup on goods is so small, where does the profit come from?
2. What exactly do members pay for, and how many of them come back every year (renewal rate)?
3. Why does selling *cheaper* make Costco *more* money? (the loop: low prices → renewals → fees)
4. Why the famous cheap hot dog combo never goes up in price (a promise, not a product).
5. What could break the model? (renewal rate falling, fee increases, competition)

## Data check
- **Costco 10-K annual reports (SEC EDGAR), Tier 1.** Total revenue, net income, and gross margin are in the
  SEC's XBRL data, so our fetcher can pull them automatically. The latest fiscal year (ending around
  August 30, 2026) is already in SEC data.
- **Membership fees: one risk.** The SEC data feed doesn't break out membership fees on their own (checked
  today), so I'll read that line directly from the income statement in each 10-K and cite the exact page.
  That's still Tier 1, just manual.
- **Renewal rate and member counts** are stated in the 10-K text ("Membership" section). Manual too.
- **Hot dog price promise:** needs a primary source (Costco's own statement or earnings-call transcript). If I
  can't find one, it becomes a light visual aside or gets cut.

## Working title and thumbnail
- Title A: **Costco Barely Profits From What It Sells. So How Does It Make Money?**
- Title B: Costco's Real Product Isn't in the Store
- Thumbnail: left, an overflowing shopping cart, greyed out with a tiny "$" over it; right, a glowing
  membership card with a big green "$". Text: **"IT'S NOT THE FOOD"**. Original drawn graphics, no logos
  copied (Costco's name in text only).

## Visual hunch
A shopping cart that empties as we look at the profit: each item shrinks to its tiny markup, and the
leftover pile is dwarfed by a single membership card. Through the video the card keeps returning as the
"hero object". Renewals become a loop of the same card going round a calendar year.

## Target length
About 10 minutes, plus 3–4 Shorts (e.g. "the hot dog promise", "the card is the product").

## Not advice
We explain the business model. No "buy Costco stock" or opinions on the share price.
